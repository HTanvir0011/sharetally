The gap

Deeds are registered at the Sub-Registry office (Law Ministry); the record of rights is updated through mutation at the AC (Land) office (Land Ministry). By law the Sub-Registrar must send a Land Transfer (L.T.) notice for every registered transfer, and the Revenue Officer must open a mutation file on receiving it (State Acquisition and Tenancy Act 1950, ss. 89 and 143C). Until mutation happens, the khatian still shows the seller.

A sale deed is registered when the seller appears in the latest khatian and swears they have not already transferred the property (Registration Act 1908, s.52A). If the first buyer has not mutated, a second sale of the same share can pass both tests.

The mechanism

  1. Start from the latest khatian shares for the dag (exact fractions; anna notation, decimals, acres and configured local units are converted).
  2. Replay every registered transfer and inheritance after the record date, oldest first. Earlier transfers keep priority, as in Transfer of Property Act 1882 s.48.
  3. No holder may pass on more than they hold. A deed asking for more raises OVER_TRANSFER with the earlier deeds as evidence.
  4. Mutation applications must cite a deed that exists in the registry index and matches it; missing or older-than-index deeds are reported as conflict or incomplete.
  5. If the digital index does not cover the whole period, or a unit, heir list or old-survey dag is ambiguous, the result is Incomplete, never "no conflict".

What it does not do

  • It does not decide ownership, validate title or label anyone fraudulent. It checks whether the supplied records are arithmetically consistent.
  • It cannot see unregistered arrangements (for example an oral partition among heirs) or deeds that never reached the index.
  • It does not compute inheritance shares; heir fractions come from the succession record.
  • Katha, bigha and the kora/kranti/til ratios vary or are disputed between sources; they are configuration for a mentor or land officer to confirm, not facts the engine assumes.
  • The registry feed, roles and accounts are simulated. No government system is accessed.

Data

All data is synthetic, generated by sharetally/synthetic.py with a fixed seed. Names carry obvious markers (Namuna, Kalponik, Udahoron, Porikkha, Kritrim), mouzas are "(DEMO)" with JL 901–903. The intake form refuses buyer names without such a marker.

AI use

Optional and never in the decision path. With an Anthropic API key, a Claude model can (1) draft a plain-Bangla notice from engine findings — rejected if it adds any number not in the facts, names anyone outside the case, or uses accusatory words — and (2) propose form fields from pasted deed text for a clerk to confirm. Without a key, deterministic templates and a rule-based reader are used.

Security on this prototype

  • PBKDF2-SHA256 password hashes, server-side sessions (HttpOnly, SameSite), CSRF tokens on every change.
  • Role checks on every page and API route: reviewer, clerk, auditor (read-only).
  • Login throttling (5 failures / 15 minutes per user and per IP).
  • Parameterised SQL, auto-escaped templates, strict Content-Security-Policy with no inline scripts.
  • Hash-chained audit log with a verification view for the auditor.
