1. Problem statement chosen

P3 — Fraud & Double-Selling Detection: detecting the same plot sold to more than one buyer, sales beyond one's share, and mismatches between registration and mutation records. Our deployment point is the e-mutation review step (the workflow of P4), but the contribution is the detection mechanism.

2. The problem in the Bangladesh context

Deeds are registered at Sub-Registry offices under the Law Ministry; the record of rights (khatian) is updated through mutation at AC (Land) offices under the Ministry of Land. The LandTech rule book names this split as a root of land disputes [1]. Under the Registration Act 1908 s.52A, a sale deed is registered when the latest khatian in the seller's name, a 25-year ownership description and the seller's affidavit that they have not transferred the property to any person are attached [2]. The khatian changes only when someone completes mutation, and many buyers do not: in a 2018 trial across 46 upazilas only about 30% of new owners applied within three months [3]; e-mutation took 43 days on average against a 28-day target in 2022 [4].

The failure. A seller sells 20 decimal; the buyer does not mutate; the khatian still shows the seller. The seller sells the same land again. The second deed passes the same khatian-plus-affidavit test, and its buyer may reach the mutation desk first. Detection today depends on an officer noticing — in 2026 reports, forged-deed mutations were caught by one AC (Land) officer's suspicion or reversed only after a manual check of the Sub-Registry volume [5].

The fix does not require a new legal flow. The Sub-Registrar must already send a Land Transfer (L.T.) notice for every registered transfer (State Acquisition and Tenancy Act 1950 s.89), and the land office must open a mutation file on receiving it (s.143C) [6]. What is missing is reconciling those notices against recorded shares. Selling land beyond one's holding is now an offence (Land Crimes Prevention and Remedy Act 2023 s.4) [7]; ShareTally helps catch it before the record is changed. Automatic mutation and data sharing between the offices were approved in 2020, but integration was still described as a plan in 2026 [8].

3. Proposed solution

ShareTally treats each plot like an account of shares. Inputs: latest khatian shares, registered transfers (digital L.T. notices), succession records, mutation applications, and the dates the digital registry index covers.

  1. Exact baseline. Anna notation (16 anna = whole), decimal shares, acres, decimals and configured local units become exact fractions — no rounding drift.
  2. Replay oldest first. Every later transfer and inheritance is applied in date order; earlier transfers keep priority, as in Transfer of Property Act 1882 s.48 [9].
  3. Conservation. Nobody can pass on more than they hold → OVER_TRANSFER, citing the khatian entry and every earlier deed.
  4. Cross-check. A mutation must cite a deed that is in the registry index and matches it → DEED_NOT_IN_INDEX, MUTATION_MISMATCH.
  5. Refuse to guess. Index gaps, undefined local units (bigha/katha), heir shares not adding up, old-survey dags split into several plots, or sellers with no recorded source → Incomplete, never "clean".

Statuses: Conflict Incomplete Attention (record lag > 90 days, quick resale — context only) No conflict in supplied records. The reviewer sees evidence, a share strip and the timeline, and records a decision; ShareTally never decides ownership or labels anyone fraudulent.

A check of the khatian alone would have cleared this case.

5. Key features

  • Mutation queue sorted by risk; each case shows why, with evidence records and a suggested action.
  • Share strip: who holds what, recorded vs now; over-sold land shown beyond 100%.
  • Pre-registration check for the Sub-Registry clerk before a deed is registered.
  • Honest statuses: coverage gaps, unknown units and ambiguous old-survey dags give Incomplete.
  • Reviewer decisions with reasons; "proceed" blocked while a conflict exists; engine version stored.
  • Tamper-evident audit log (hash chain) with auditor verification.
  • Bangla + English explanations; optional AI notice draft rejected if it adds unsupported numbers or accusations.
  • Fictional-data guard: new names without a fictional marker are refused.

6. Evaluation and limitations

Eighteen hand-written scenarios (double sale, co-heir over-sale, forged-deed citation, deed older than the index, quantity mismatch, rapid resale, unknown seller, coverage gap, split old dag, undefined bigha, inheritance, incomplete heir list, over-allocated khatian, anna notation, rounding, sale after selling everything, record lag) plus 30 generated clean plots. Expected results were declared next to the data before the engine ran.

Measure (synthetic, demo clock 2026-10-01)Result
Declared statuses and finding codes matched (plots + cases)70 / 70
Clean background plots wrongly flagged0 / 30
Incomplete-data cases reported as clean0 / 13
Problem cases a khatian-only check would have cleared8 / 12
Slowest single-plot ledger (laptop)< 1 ms

Honest reading: the same team wrote the rules and the expectations, so this shows the mechanism works as designed, not real-world accuracy. Limitations: needs a complete digital transfer feed (historic paper deeds give Incomplete until digitised); unregistered family partitions may appear as conflicts (reviewer can mark "explained"); heir fractions come from succession records (no faraid calculation); katha/bigha and kora–kranti–til ratios vary between sources and are configuration; the old-survey crosswalk is a lookup table, not a map overlay; registry integration is simulated.

Governance: rules versioned and owned by the Ministry of Land; every decision stores the engine version. Scale: each plot is computed independently, so the check scales horizontally. Privacy: role-based access, audit logging, no personal data sent to AI (tokens instead of names). Main risk: inter-ministry data sharing; mitigated by starting from the notice the law already requires.

সমস্যা: দলিল নিবন্ধন হয় সাব-রেজিস্ট্রি অফিসে, খতিয়ান হালনাগাদ হয় এসি ল্যান্ড অফিসে নামজারির মাধ্যমে। ক্রেতা নামজারি না করা পর্যন্ত খতিয়ানে বিক্রেতার নামই থাকে, তাই একই অংশ আবার বিক্রি হলে দ্বিতীয় দলিলও একই যাচাইয়ে পার হয়ে যেতে পারে।

সমাধান: ShareTally প্রতিটি দাগকে অংশের হিসাব-খাতা হিসেবে দেখে — খতিয়ান দিয়ে শুরু, তারপর আইন অনুযায়ী আগে থেকেই পাঠানো এল.টি. নোটিশের প্রতিটি নিবন্ধিত দলিল পুরোনো থেকে নতুন ক্রমে বসানো হয়। কেউ অবশিষ্ট অংশের বেশি হস্তান্তর করলে আগের দলিলসহ প্রমাণ দেখিয়ে "দ্বন্দ্ব" জানানো হয়; তথ্য অসম্পূর্ণ হলে ফলাফল "অসম্পূর্ণ", কখনো "পরিষ্কার" নয়। চূড়ান্ত সিদ্ধান্ত নেন সহকারী কমিশনার (ভূমি); সফটওয়্যার মালিকানা নির্ধারণ করে না এবং কাউকে প্রতারক বলে না। প্রোটোটাইপের সব তথ্য কাল্পনিক।
